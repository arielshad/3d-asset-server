An overcast HDRI is a panorama taken under a fully clouded sky. The clouds act as one huge softbox, so the light is even, slightly cool and almost shadowless. That makes it the most forgiving environment for checking a model or material: nothing is hidden in a hard shadow and nothing is blown out by direct sun.

The HDRIs here come from Poly Haven, ambientCG, BlenderKit and other libraries, found by one live search and refreshed automatically. Most are CC0.

## Good uses

- **Look development.** Neutral light shows a material's true colour and roughness, so many artists check textures under an overcast sky before trying moodier lighting.
- **Photoscanned assets.** Scans are captured under diffuse light. Rendering them under an overcast HDRI keeps them consistent with the light they were shot in.
- **Rainy or northern scenes.** Wet streets, coastlines and forests read as more believable under grey light than under a clear blue sky.

Because there is no sun to aim, lighting is less about rotation and more about exposure: raise or lower the strength until whites stay white without clipping.
